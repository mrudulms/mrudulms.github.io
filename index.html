About

I develop and apply ab initio and model-based methods to understand light-driven dynamics in solids. My work connects attosecond science, where I helped establish high-harmonic generation as a probe of defects, valleys and topology in two-dimensional and quantum materials, with ultrafast magnetism, where I study how angular momentum is created and shared among spin, orbital, phonon and magnon degrees of freedom on femtosecond timescales.

I received my Ph.D. in Physics from IIT Bombay (2021) and have been a postdoctoral researcher with Prof. Peter M. Oppeneer at Uppsala University since 2022. I am now building an independent research group on the light-driven control of angular momentum in quantum materials.

Research

Ultrafast angular-momentum dynamics in magnets

Where does angular momentum go when a laser pulse demagnetizes a ferromagnet? I study its transfer from spins and orbitals to the lattice and to magnons: the generation of phonons that carry angular momentum, chiral phonons from magnon–phonon coupling, and phonon-controlled topological magnons.

Key work: Phys. Rev. Lett. 135 (2025); Phys. Rev. B 112 (2025, Editors’ Suggestion)

Orbital and spin magnetism from light

Separating the spin and orbital responses of materials to circularly polarized femtosecond pulses, and orbital transport by magnons in altermagnets — a route towards magnon orbitronics.

Key work: npj Quantum Mater. 11 (2026); J. Phys. B (2026); Phys. Rev. B 109 (2024)

Attosecond high-harmonic spectroscopy

Using high-harmonic generation as an all-optical probe of defects, real-space electron recollision, lattice dynamics, topology and twisted bilayers, and designing tailored light fields that control valley polarization in graphene.

Key work: Optica 8 (2021, top-cited); npj Comput. Mater. 6 (2020); Phys. Rev. B 110 (2024)

Future research programme

My group will develop first-principles and quantum-kinetic methods to describe and control the flow of angular momentum between electrons, spins, magnons and phonons under ultrafast excitation, along three directions:

  1. Light-driven control of altermagnets, using their spin-split bands and magnons as a platform for femtosecond spin and orbital currents.
  2. Chiral phonon and magnon engineering, using phonon angular momentum to switch and steer magnetic and topological states.
  3. Attosecond spectroscopy of magnetic order, extending high-harmonic spectroscopy from non-magnetic 2D materials to magnets.

The programme connects naturally to experimental ultrafast-optics and THz groups, and trains students across electronic-structure theory, nonlinear optics and high-performance computing.
